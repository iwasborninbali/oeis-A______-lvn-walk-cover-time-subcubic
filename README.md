## Definition

For n ≥ 1, a(n) is the maximum number of moves before first coverage, taken over all connected simple graphs with maximum degree at most three on n vertices, every start vertex, and every legal tie choice. The offset is 1; a(1)=0.

All graphs are finite, undirected, connected, and simple. A walk starts at any vertex x₀, whose counter is initially 1; all other counters are 0. At each step choose any neighbor with the smallest current visit count, move to it, and increment its count. Ties may depend on the entire history; there is no waiting or reset. Coverage is the first time all counters are positive. The first-coverage time is measured in edge moves. For the visit-count sequence the maximum is over t < T, with the counted initial visit included; the empty range for a singleton gives 0. The two objectives are optimized separately.

## How it was obtained

Graph representatives are generated by connected vertex augmentation, followed by canonical-label isomorphism rejection. Every connected graph can be reduced to a connected smaller graph by deleting a leaf of a spanning tree. On each graph the producer uses a memoized state search over all starts and all minimum-counter moves. Its recorded per-graph outputs and catalogues are included. Trees are identified by n−1 edges, cubic graphs by degree exactly 3 at each vertex. See `verification/compare_replay.py` for table extraction.

The finite search terminates: if u and v are adjacent, while c(u) ≤ k, v can receive at most deg(v)(k+1)+1 visits. Starting at an unvisited vertex and propagating along paths bounds all counts before coverage. There is no assumption of a fixed tie rule.

## Verification

The producer was rerun on the supplied catalogue through 12 vertices and compared per graph. A separately written audit generates its own graph representatives and recomputes all extrema through 9 vertices; it also replays the two 12-vertex global witnesses. The independent catalogue and separately written C++ state search were also replayed through 12 vertices and matched every graph and both extrema to the producer by isomorphism. ## Reproduce

From the repository root on a machine with Python 3 and a C++17 compiler:

```sh
sh verification/replay_producer.sh
sh verification/replay_independent.sh
(cd verification && python3 independent_census.py --max-n 9)
```

The first prints one producer JSON record and one matching-table line per n through 12; the second generates a fresh catalogue, searches it and matches all graphs, and the third prints nine census rows and two legal witness results. Approximate wall times on the replay machine: 1–2 minutes for the producer and about seven minutes total for the three independent stages, plus under 15 seconds for the sample; individual stages each remain below ten minutes on this machine. The full audit regenerates the included catalogue and result files; the sample writes `independent_graph_results.jsonl` and `independent_results.json` in `verification/`, which can be removed afterwards. For a quick check of all stored global witnesses, run `python3 verification/check.py --mode witnesses --max-n 12 --results data/exact`. See the certificate for observed results and restrictions.
